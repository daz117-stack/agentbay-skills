## 1. Executive summary

**What this plan optimizes for:** Getting the first 10 paying clients in one or two Cairo neighbourhoods, with at least 3 of them on a monthly plan and documented as permission-granted cases, without spending money on anything that depends on a name that hasn't been cleared yet.

**Three bets, ranked by leverage**

1. **Win a neighbourhood, not a market.** Small F&B and retail owners buy on what they see next door and on who recommends you. Concentrating the first 10 clients in 1–2 neighbourhoods makes every delivered menu, screen and window sticker visible to the next prospect. Field visits with a free menu draft beat any online channel at this stage, because the buyer is standing in the shop, not searching.
2. **Sell one productized offer before selling the full range.** The brand promises looks + runs + stays, but delivering custom order and stock systems to the first clients would break a two-person team. Launch with a fixed-scope "Menu setup" package (designed menu, digital screen or QR version, printed version, price updates on a monthly plan) and add order and stock screens as named add-ons once the menu offer is repeatable. This keeps the "affordable" promise profitable.
3. **Make WhatsApp the storefront.** The conversion action, the sales conversation, delivery updates and support all happen on WhatsApp. A tidy WhatsApp Business setup (catalogue with package prices, quick replies, labels as a pipeline) is worth more in the first 90 days than a full website. The website is a one-page credibility check that sends people to WhatsApp.

**What 12 months plausibly looks like**
- Name cleared (or switched to the fallback) before any printed or paid asset ships.
- 20–30 setups delivered, 10–15 on monthly plans **[confirm against founder capacity, §13]**.
- 3–5 documented cases, used with permission, in Arabic and English.
- Referrals (sticker, credit line, owner-to-owner) bringing in at least a third of new clients.
- A tested second package (order screen or stock screen) with its own go-live checklist and fallback.
- A decision, backed by numbers, on whether and when to test the Gulf.

**90-day priorities**
1. Clear the name, register handles, and set up WhatsApp Business (weeks 1–2).
2. Decide the launch package, its price and the written support time (weeks 1–2).
3. Run 8–10 owner conversations; rewrite customer language from what they say (weeks 1–4).
4. Sign 3–5 founding clients at founding prices in exchange for case permission (weeks 3–8).
5. Ship the one-page site, Google Business Profile and Instagram/Facebook with real work only (weeks 3–6).
6. Sell a Ramadan menu setup to existing contacts and neighbours before Ramadan starts (weeks 9–12).

## 2. Strategic frame

### What Firststone is, in one sentence
Firststone is the affordable setup partner that gets small cafés, restaurants and shops looking right and running right, and stays to keep them that way. (`positioning.md`)

### The category we're claiming
A new category, "the setup partner". At the moment of decision the owner compares a freelancer, a platform subscription, or doing nothing. Firststone sits in the unclaimed corner of the map: an ongoing partner for both looks and systems. Marketing uses the category in copy and uses the owner's search words (digital menu, منيو ديجيتال, menu design, a system for orders) in SEO, Google Business Profile and ads.

### Who we're for
- Independent cafés, restaurants, bakeries, mini-markets and shops, 1–5 branches, Greater Cairo first.
- The owner decides, usually on the floor; price-aware; Arabic-first.
- Stated problem: "my menu looks bad / prices are wrong". Real problem: no one reliable owns how the place looks and runs.
- What they're actually buying: relief from chasing suppliers, and looking like the bigger places.

### Business-model logic
Setup packages are the entry. Monthly plans are the business. Each retained client is also a visible asset in its neighbourhood (screens, menus, sticker), which makes referral the compounding channel. The moat is thin in year 1 (`positioning.md` §03), so **retention is the strategy** and the plan treats churn as a top metric from client 1.

### Market-quality gate (problem size × frequency)
- **Frequency: high.** Menus, prices and offers change constantly (inflation-driven price changes, seasonal menus, daily specials); order handling happens every shift.
- **Size: medium.** Painful at rush hour and at price changes, but owners have survived on paper for years.
- **Quadrant:** small-to-medium problem, high frequency. Habit-forming but price-sensitive and easy to churn. Implication: price the monthly plan around the frequent job (keeping prices and menus right), make it cheap to stay, and keep acquisition cost near zero through referrals and field sales.

### Brand voice non-negotiables
- Plain, calm, exact, warm, honest, practical (`voice.md`).
- Egyptian colloquial for social, WhatsApp and sales; light MSA for contracts; English for website and decks.
- No exclamation marks in product, support or billing. Numerals for prices and times. Sentence case.
- Never: "best in Egypt", "guaranteed sales", "100% uptime", "cheapest", "digital transformation", POS/ERP in headlines, "FirstStone".
- No proof claim before it is true.

## 4. Acquisition

### Current state
Nothing live. No handles, no site, no listings.

### Moves
**Move 1 — Neighbourhood field sales (primary).** Pick 1–2 neighbourhoods where the founders can visit regularly **[NEED: choose; the story draft uses Heliopolis as a placeholder]**. Walk in during off-peak hours (10:30–12:00, 16:00–18:00), ask for the owner, and offer one concrete thing: "Send us a photo of your menu. We'll send a free draft by Thursday." Carry a tablet with the brand film and one live demo menu. Log every visit as a WhatsApp label (§5). Target: 15–20 visits a week once the offer is priced. *Skills: `sales-enablement` (leave-behind, later), `copywriting`.*

**Move 2 — Free menu draft (the lead magnet).** The only free thing Firststone gives away: a one-page redesign draft of the owner's current menu, in Firststone's system, delivered on WhatsApp within 3 working days. It proves "well-designed" in the owner's own context and opens the sales conversation. Cap it to protect capacity (e.g., 5 drafts a week). *Skills: `offers`, `copywriting`.*

**Move 3 — Google Business Profile.** Owners and partners will check the name on Google Maps. Create the profile once the name is cleared, with the category nearest to "graphic designer / marketing agency / software company" **[check available categories]**, real photos of delivered work, prices in the description, and the WhatsApp number. Ask every client for a review after go-live (§7; no incentives for reviews). *Skills: `referrals` (review programs).*

**Move 4 — Instagram and Facebook, work-first.** Both are where Cairo owners look at other shops. Post only real work: before/after menus, a screen being installed, a 10-second clip of a price update showing on every screen at once. Launch with the brand film. Bio and greeting lines from `messaging.md` §06. Cadence: 3 posts a week, Egyptian colloquial. *Skills: `social` (not vendored yet; add when this starts), `copywriting`.*

**Move 5 — Partners as referrers.** Hardware sellers (tablets, screens), POS resellers and print shops get asked for design and setup they don't offer. Offer a simple referral fee or reciprocal referrals (§7). Start with 3 conversations in the chosen neighbourhoods. *Skills: `referrals`, `co-marketing` (later).*

**Move 6 — One-page website.** A credibility check, not a channel. Hero, the three-part promise, packages with prices, how it works, the founders, WhatsApp button. Spec and copy in `04-website-copy.md`. *Skills: `copywriting`, `site-architecture`.*

**Move 7 — Paid (held).** Click-to-WhatsApp ads on Facebook/Instagram targeting business owners in the chosen neighbourhoods are the right first paid test, because they open a free WhatsApp entry window (`05-whatsapp-playbook.md`). Hold until: name cleared, packages priced, 3 cases published. Then test with a small fixed budget and a kill rule (cost per qualified WhatsApp conversation). *Skills: `ads`, `ad-creative` (add when unlocked).*

**Skipped for now, with reason:** SEO content program (owners don't search for this category yet; revisit in Q3 for "digital menu Cairo"-type terms), PR (nothing to announce until cases exist), events, influencer marketing (wrong audience), cold email (owners don't live in email), Product Hunt and directories (not this buyer).

## 5. Activation

"Activation" for a service means: a prospect gets a first valued experience and becomes a paying client.

**The path:** visit or post → WhatsApp message → free draft (3 working days) → 20-minute follow-up visit with the draft on a tablet and a written package quote → deposit → setup → go-live with staff training → monthly plan.

**Moves**
- **WhatsApp as pipeline.** Labels for each stage: `visited`, `draft requested`, `draft sent`, `quoted`, `won`, `live`, `on plan`, `lost`. Weekly count per label is the funnel report until a CRM is justified. *Skill: `sms` (WhatsApp).*
- **Quote within 24 hours of the draft review.** Written, fixed scope, one page, in Arabic, price in numerals. Template in `02-offer-and-pricing.md`.
- **Go-live checklist as an activation asset.** Show it before the sale. It turns "built to run every day" from a claim into something the owner can read. Fady writes it, following change-management practice (test, rollback/fallback, handover, sign-off).
- **First-week check-in.** Day 2 and day 7 WhatsApp check-ins after go-live, using the support voice ("Got your message. We're looking at it now and will reply before 3.").

## 6. Retention

The plan's real business. Every move here protects monthly plan revenue.

- **Monthly plan = the frequent job.** Price changes, daily specials, seasonal menus and a set number of content pieces per month. The owner should feel the plan every week, not only when something breaks.
- **Monthly "what changed" message.** First working day of each month, a short WhatsApp note: what was updated, what's coming (e.g., Ramadan menu), one suggestion based on their sales if they share it. This is the retention touchpoint and the upsell moment.
- **Seasonal calendar.** Ramadan (expected 8 or 9 Feb 2027; Dar al-Ifta confirms after the moon sighting), Eid al-Fitr, summer, back-to-school, Eid al-Adha, New Year. Each gets a pre-built template set so updates are fast and cheap to deliver.
- **Exit without friction.** If a client leaves, hand over their files and data on request (the "no lock-in" promise). Ask one question on exit and log the reason; churn reasons go into `../.agents/product-marketing.md` Learnings once patterns repeat.
- *Skills: `sms` (WhatsApp flows), `churn-prevention` (add once there are 10+ plans).*

## 7. Referral

The cheapest channel Firststone has, and already designed into the identity.

- **Window sticker** «متجهّز مع Firststone» on every client that agrees, plus a small "Made by Firststone" credit on delivered menus and screen footers.
- **Owner-to-owner referral reward** paid in service, not cash: one free month of plan for the referrer when the referred shop goes live.
- **Google reviews** requested after go-live and again after the first month. Never offer anything in exchange for a review; Google's policies prohibit incentivized reviews.
- **Partner referrals** from hardware sellers and print shops (§4 Move 5).
- Full design: `06-referral-program.md`.

## 8. Revenue

- **Packages:** one launch package (Menu setup), two named add-ons (Order screen, Stock screen) introduced once each has a go-live checklist and a fallback, and two monthly plans. Full structure in `02-offer-and-pricing.md`.
- **Founding-client price:** the first 5 clients pay a founding price, locked for 12 months, in exchange for written permission to document and publish their case. This is real scarcity (5 slots, capacity-limited) and the fastest route to proof.
- **Price method:** pick a price you can learn from, publish it, raise it for new clients once the founding slots fill and owners stop pushing back. Use the interview questions in `03-customer-interviews.md` to test price ranges before setting them.

### Unit economics
| Metric | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Average monthly plan revenue per client | **[NEED]** | Set with packages |
| Setup revenue per client | **[NEED]** | Set with packages |
| Cost to acquire (founder hours × hourly value + free drafts + printing) | **[NEED]** | Track hours per won client from day 1 |
| Monthly plan retention | **[NEED]** | Track from client 1 |
| Delivery hours per setup and per plan-month | **[NEED]** | The number that decides whether "affordable" is profitable |

Track delivery hours from the first client; without them, pricing is guesswork and the "affordable but well-designed" tension (`brand-context.md`) can't be managed.

## 10. 12-month outlook

**Budget method:** goal-based, but CAC is unknown, so no formula is applied yet. Spend stays near zero until Q2; the first paid test is gated on proof (Q2). Growth expectation: linear, a few clients a month, capped by founder delivery hours rather than demand.

**Q1 (Nov–Jan) — Prove the menu package.** 5–10 clients, 3+ on plans, name cleared, prices published, checklist used on every go-live. KPIs: visits → drafts → quotes → wins conversion; hours per setup; zero missed support-time commitments.

**Q2 (Feb–Apr) — Ramadan and proof.** Deliver the Ramadan wave; publish 3 cases; launch the referral reward; first click-to-WhatsApp ad test in the chosen neighbourhoods with a kill rule. KPIs: referral share of new clients; plan retention at 90 days; cost per qualified WhatsApp conversation.

**Q3 (May–Jul) — Second package.** Introduce Order screen *or* Stock screen (whichever interviews and clients ask for most) after verifying e-invoicing/e-receipt obligations and writing its fallback. Start light SEO for local search terms if the site has steady traffic. KPIs: add-on attach rate; plan revenue share of total revenue.

**Q4 (Aug–Oct) — Decide the next curve.** With 20–30 clients' data: decide whether to add a third neighbourhood, hire delivery help, raise prices, or test the Gulf. KPIs: 12-month plan retention; revenue per founder hour.

## 13. Measurement, RACI, open decisions

**North star (proposed):** number of clients on a monthly plan. It captures the business-model thesis (setup is entry, plans are the business) and is hard to game.

| Stage | Leading indicators |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | Visits per week; drafts requested; WhatsApp conversations started |
| Activation | Draft → quote %; quote → win %; days from first contact to go-live |
| Retention | Plans active at 30/90 days; support replies within the promised time |
| Referral | % of new clients referred; stickers installed; Google reviews |
| Revenue | Revenue per founder hour; setup vs plan revenue mix |

**Cadence:** weekly 30-minute founder review of WhatsApp label counts and hours; monthly update of `product-marketing.md` Learnings; plan review at day 90.

| Domain | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted |

**Open decisions blocking the plan (ranked)**
1. **Founder hours per week.** Decides support time, visits per week and how many clients can be served. Every target above scales with it.
2. **Hamdy's role and background.** Decides who owns design, content and field sales, and fills the story slots.
3. **Name clearance.** Blocks printing, Google profile, stickers and ads.
4. **Package scope and prices.** Blocks quotes, website and WhatsApp catalogue.
5. **Support response time.** Blocks the "support you can reach" proof.
6. **Employer policy.** Blocks any public mention of Fady's background.
7. **E-invoicing / e-receipt obligations.** Blocks the order-screen package (not the menu package).
8. **Neighbourhood choice.** Blocks field-sales routing.
