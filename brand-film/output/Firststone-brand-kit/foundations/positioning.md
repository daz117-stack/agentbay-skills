# Firststone: Positioning

Built with the `brand-positioning` skill from `brand-context.md`. Competitor facts come from desk research (Oct 2026). Re-verify before external use.

## 01 — Category definition

- **Stated category:** a digital agency plus a small software house.
- **Actual category:** the owner doesn't compare "agencies". At the moment of decision they compare:
  1. a **freelancer** (a menu design or QR menu for a few hundred pounds),
  2. a **platform subscription** (Foodics, BIM POS, a QR-menu SaaS), or
  3. **doing nothing** (paper, WhatsApp, the old printed menu).
- **Opportunity category:** **the business-setup partner for small businesses.** That's the team that sets up how the place *looks* and how it *runs*, then stays to keep it working. Neither freelancers (they leave) nor platforms (they only do one job) occupy it.

**Recommendation:** Compete as *"the setup partner"*, not as an agency or a POS vendor. It explains why one company does menus, ads and order systems. It also matches the name: Firststone lays the first stone *and* builds on it.

## 02 — Competitive landscape map

**Axis 1:** One-off delivery ←→ Ongoing partner
**Axis 2:** How the business *looks* (media) ←→ How the business *runs* (systems)

| Player | Axis 1 | Axis 2 | Territory they own |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freelancers (Khamsat, Mostaql, Instagram designers) | One-off | Looks (plus basic QR menus) | Cheapest, fastest. Weak on reliability and support after delivery. |
| Small social-media agencies / print shops | One-off / light retainer | Looks | Content volume, posts. No operational systems. |
| QR-menu vendors (eMenu, QRMenuPro, Billqode) | Ongoing (subscription) | Middle: the menu only | One product, sales-led, narrow. |
| Foodics, BIM POS / Compuware | Ongoing (subscription) | Runs | Powerful standardised POS, payments, analytics. Priced and built for growth-stage F&B, not for design or marketing. |
| elmenus | Ongoing | Looks: discovery and visibility | Consumer reach. Not the business's own tools. |
| **Firststone** | **Ongoing partner** | **Both looks and runs** | **Unclaimed:** one affordable team for the whole setup, still there next month. |

## 03 — Positioning territory

**The Space:** *Starting right.* Firststone is the solid first step a small business takes from improvised to organised, in how it looks and how it runs.

**The Audience Owned:** Independent cafés, restaurants, bakeries and neighbourhood supermarkets/shops in Greater Cairo with 1–5 branches, whose owner is ready to stop running on paper and WhatsApp but won't pay enterprise prices.

**The Competitive Moat** (honest, early-stage): The moat is the *combination*, not any single capability. Design, small systems and after-delivery support from one small team is hard for freelancers to match on reliability, and for platforms to match on design and flexibility. In year one the moat is thin. It thickens through two things: retained clients, whose menus, ads and systems are all connected through you, and a reference base in a few neighbourhoods. **Retention is the strategy.**

## 04 — Positioning statement

**Framework version:**
*For owners of independent cafés, restaurants and shops who are running on paper, WhatsApp and improvised design, **Firststone** is the business-setup partner that builds how the business looks and how it runs (menus, ads, orders and stock) and keeps it working, because one team designs, builds and supports everything, with clear package prices sized for small businesses.*

**Strategic version (internal):**
*Win small F&B and retail owners at the moment they decide to "get organised", with productised setup packages (look + system), then keep them on a monthly support and content plan. Compete against freelancers on reliability and against platforms on design, flexibility and price, never on features.*

**Human version (public):**
*"You run the place. We set up how it looks and how it works, and we stay to keep it that way."*

## 05 — Proof points

Only claim these once they are true. Most are **commitments to set up before launch**, not existing facts.

- **Proof 1: One team, end to end.** The same people design the menu, build the screen or order system it runs on, and support it. *(True by design.)*
- **Proof 2: Published package prices.** Fixed setup packages and monthly plans, written scope, no hidden extras. *(Decide packages and prices.)*
- **Proof 3: Built to run every day.** Every system is tested before go-live, documented, handed over with staff training, and has a defined fallback if the internet drops. *(Define a delivery checklist; verify offline behaviour per product.)*
- **Proof 4: Support you can reach.** A written response commitment, e.g. same business day on WhatsApp. *(Define a realistic SLA for a two-person team.)*
- **Proof 5: Real local work.** 3+ documented Cairo clients, used with permission. *(Year-1 goal; none yet, so no client claims until then.)*
- **Proof 6: Founder discipline.** One founder runs IT service management and change control professionally. *(See decision note below before using publicly.)*

> **Decision for the founders:** Mentioning the banking background publicly ("built by people who run IT for banks") is a strong trust signal, but check the employer's outside-business and public-communication policy first.

## 06 — What Firststone refuses to be

- Firststone is **not the cheapest option**. It won't race freelancers to the bottom; it wins on still being there next month.
- Firststone will **never sell enterprise ERP or features a shop doesn't need**. If a spreadsheet solves it, it'll say so.
- Firststone is **not a growth-hacking agency**. No promises of viral reach or guaranteed sales numbers.
- Firststone **doesn't lock clients in**. Their data, designs and files are theirs to take.

## 07 — Positioning in one sentence

**Firststone is the affordable setup partner that gets small cafés, restaurants and shops looking right and running right, and stays to keep them that way.** (25 words)

## Red-flag check

- *Could a competitor say this?* Partly. "Affordable + reliable" alone is generic. The differentiation is the **looks + runs + stays** combination, so every message must show all three.
- *Audience too broad?* Kept tight: independent F&B and retail, 1–5 branches, Greater Cairo first. Gulf comes later as a second market, not in the launch message.
- *Table stakes as differentiators?* "Good support" alone is a table stake. It only works as proof when it's specific (a written response time, a handover checklist).
- *Can it be substantiated?* Not yet. Proofs 2–5 must be built before the public launch.
