# Naming: strategy, all directions, evaluation

Produced with the `brand-naming` skill (generation mode) from `.agents/brand-context.md`. Conflict notes come from light web screening (Oct 2026). **That screening is not a trademark search.**

## Naming Strategy

**Brand in one line:** One affordable partner that makes small businesses look good and run smoothly, with digital menus, ads, and simple order and stock systems that just work.

**Audience:** Owners of cafés, restaurants, supermarkets and shops in Egypt, then the Gulf. Hands-on, price-aware, Arabic-first, comfortable with English tech words.

**Positioning angle:** Dependable and well-made, without the agency price. "Done right, every day."

**Emotional territory:** Relief and confidence. "These people get it right, and they're people like me." Calm precision with a warm handshake.

**Pronunciation constraints:** No P or V (Arabic speakers substitute B/F). Avoid hard clusters for English readers. One obvious Latin spelling. Must also be writable cleanly in Arabic script.

**Naming directions explored:**
1. Everyday Arabic words: warm, instantly understood
2. Arabic roots of structure and craft: stability, mastery
3. Bilingual double meanings: one word, a story in both languages
4. Plain English words of craft and precision
5. Coined forms from Arabic roots: easier to own

**Themes / words to explore:** precision, "just right", foundation, corner/cornerstone, pillar, support, balance and fair measure (honest trade), mastery of work (itqan), the drawn line, two pieces joining.

**Themes / words to avoid:** -ify / -ly / -io / -hub / -labs / -tech suffixes, "smart", "digital", "solutions", "nexus", generic AI-sounding coinages. Names that box the company into one product (menus only, POS only). Names close to Foodics / elmenus / Square.

## Direction 1: Everyday Arabic words
Words the customer already says every day, so the brand feels familiar before the first meeting.

**Mazboot (مضبوط / colloquial مظبوط)**
Meaning: "Exactly right, properly adjusted, correct." In Egypt it is also how you order coffee with just-right sugar (*ahwa mazboot*).
Why it works: It says *precise* and *warm* at the same time. That's the brand personality in one word. It gives an instant story for the core café/restaurant segment and is understood across Egypt, the Levant and the Gulf.
Tagline direction: "Mazboot. Done right, every day."
Risk / caution: Common word, so legal distinctiveness has to be checked (likely registrable in software/advertising classes as a stylised mark; confirm with counsel). Latin spelling varies (Mazboot / Mazbout / Mazbut), so pick one and hold the others. Standard Arabic spelling is مضبوط; مظبوط is the colloquial spelling, which is a deliberate choice for an Arabic logotype. Café businesses may already use it as a shop name (food & beverage class, different from yours).
Score: 8.5/10

**Tamam (تمام)**
Meaning: "All good / complete / perfect."
Why it works: The warmest, most universal word in the set.
Tagline direction: "Tamam, it's handled."
Risk / caution: **Tamam is Zain KSA's licensed micro-finance app (3.4M+ downloads).** That's a strong collision for Gulf expansion. It's also unsearchable as a bare word.
Score: 6.5/10

**Sahl (سهل)**
Meaning: "Easy."
Why it works: Directly answers "keep it simple".
Risk / caution: Undersells precision and craft. Heavily used in Gulf government and fintech naming.
Score: 6/10

**Dukkan (دكان)**
Meaning: "The shop."
Why it works: Very warm and local.
Risk / caution: Too narrow (retail only, not media or software) and crowded in e-commerce.
Score: 5.5/10

## Direction 2: Arabic roots of structure and craft
Words that carry stability and mastery, giving gravitas without coldness.

**Rukn / Rokn (ركن)**
Meaning: "Corner" *and* "pillar / cornerstone" (أركان = the pillars of something).
Why it works: It ties to the L-shaped corner mark and to the three pillars. It's short, solid and serious.
Tagline direction: "Rokn. The corner your business stands on."
Risk / caution: The final cluster -kn is awkward for English readers. The spelling splits (Rukn / Rokn / Rukun); Rokn matches Egyptian pronunciation. No direct conflict found in a first screen.
Score: 7.5/10

**Sanad (سند)**
Meaning: "Support, backing, someone you can lean on."
Why it works: Trusted and warm. A partner, not a vendor.
Risk / caution: Very common in Gulf institutional names, so it's likely crowded. Feels more like a service or insurance company than a design-led studio.
Score: 6.5/10

**Itqan (إتقان)**
Meaning: Mastery, doing work perfectly (a deeply respected work value).
Why it works: Exactly "well-designed" as a value.
Risk / caution: **Crowded in your exact category:** ITQAN (Jeddah IT/software), ITQAN (Abu Dhabi integrator), Etqan Tech (Cairo IT solutions).
Score: 6/10

**Mizan (ميزان)**
Meaning: Scales, balance, fair measure.
Why it works: Honest trade, transparent prices.
Risk / caution: Crowded (banking and finance naming). Reads as finance, not media.
Score: 6/10

## Direction 3: Bilingual double meanings
One word with a story in both languages.

**Keel / Kayl (كيل)**
Meaning: English *keel*, the backbone that keeps a ship steady. Arabic *kayl*, the honest measure in trade.
Why it works: Stable + Trusted in one syllable.
Risk / caution: The English idiom "keel over" means *collapse*, which is fatal for a brand built on stability. Also thin meaning for media.
Score: 6/10

**Zawya (زاوية)**
Meaning: "Corner / angle", and in Egypt the corner shop.
Why it works: The corner mark, the local shop.
Risk / caution: **Zawya is an established MENA business-news brand (LSEG).** High conflict.
Score: 4.5/10

**Lamma (لمّة)**
Meaning: Egyptian for "a gathering, bringing things together".
Why it works: Warm. It suggests connecting media and systems.
Risk / caution: Too colloquial and soft for software. Means nothing in English, and Gulf recognition is weaker.
Score: 5.5/10

## Direction 4: Plain English words of craft and precision

**True Line**
Meaning: In carpentry, *true* = perfectly straight. It also picks up the film's line motif.
Why it works: Precise, honest, and it connects directly to the existing brand film.
Risk / caution: Two words. "Trueline" is very common worldwide, so hard to own. English-only meaning.
Score: 6.5/10

**Steady**
Meaning: Dependable, consistent.
Why it works: The Stable pillar, literally.
Risk / caution: Flat in Arabic, and generic. Existing fintech brands use it.
Score: 5.5/10

**Corner**
Meaning: The corner shop, the corner mark.
Risk / caution: Generic and unownable.
Score: 5/10

**Plumb**
Meaning: Perfectly vertical; exact.
Risk / caution: Arabic speakers say "Blumb", which fails the sound test.
Score: 4/10

## Direction 5: Coined from Arabic roots

**Rokna (ركنة)**
Meaning: Softened coinage from *rokn* (corner / pillar).
Why it works: Easier to say and spell in English than Rokn, probably easier to own, and keeps the cornerstone story.
Risk / caution: In Egyptian, *rokna* also reads as "a little corner / nook", which is cosy rather than solid. The meaning needs explaining.
Score: 7/10

**Mazboota (مظبوطة)**
Meaning: Feminine form of Mazboot.
Risk / caution: Sounds like a product or café name, not a company. Weaker than the root.
Score: 5.5/10

# Round 2: English names carrying the meaning of Rokn / Rokna

Founder brief (round 2): *"Something that means Rokn or Rokna but in English, in the context of brand marketing and small ERP & IT services."*
Rokn (ركن) = corner + pillar / cornerstone, the solid point a structure stands on. Rokna = the softer "corner / nook".

## Naming Strategy (round 2)

**Brand in one line:** The cornerstone partner for small businesses: brand marketing plus small ERP and IT systems that hold the business up.
**Emotional territory:** Solid, dependable, built properly, but approachable, not corporate.
**Directions:** (1) the cornerstone itself, (2) the main support / pillar, (3) structural joinery (two pieces forming a corner), (4) coined compounds of *corner*.
**Constraints kept:** English, no P/V sounds, one obvious spelling, distinct from crowded "Cornerstone / Keystone / Angle" names and from regional ERP/POS vendors.

## Round-2 recommendation: **Cornerline**

- **Meaning:** *corner* is Rokn (the corner, the cornerstone you build on); *line* is precision, the drawn line, and a line of services. Said together: "the corner your business is built on, drawn properly."
- **Fits both halves of the offer.** Brand marketing (lines, layout, design) and small ERP/IT (structure, systems, a "line" of business). It doesn't lock the company into menus or POS.
- **Zero identity rework.** The temporary mark (two L-strokes forming a corner) and the film's "load-bearing line" concept *are* a corner made of lines. Name, mark and film tell one story.
- **Pronunciation.** Easy for Arabic speakers (no P/V), one spelling. Arabic transliteration كورنرلاين; the story line in Arabic: «ركن شغلك… مرسوم صح».
- **Honest caveats.** It's a coined compound, so the meaning is felt rather than instantly literal. The first screen found no company using it, but **domain (.com/.eg/.sa/.ae), handles and trademark (Egypt ITDA, Classes 9/35/42) still need checking**. Mainstay is the fallback if Cornerline fails clearance.

# Evaluation: founder proposal "StartIT"

Founder's intent: *"Start here: you can't start your service or IT without our support and services; IT for IT services."*

**StartIT**

What it suggests: A startup programme, an IT training course, or an IT-only services firm.
What it makes people feel: Energetic, beginner-ish. "Starting out", not "built to last".
Category fit: Fits the IT half only. The brand-marketing and media half (menus, ads, campaigns) disappears from the name.
Premium or cheap feel: Generic. Reads like a course or a programme rather than a company you'd trust with daily operations.
Easy to say: Yes, but the pun is lost in speech. Spoken, it's "start it"; the "IT" only exists in the capitalisation.
Easy to spell: Depends. StartIT / Startit / Start-IT / Start IT will all appear.
Logo potential: Moderate. The capitalised "IT" carries the logo and pushes the brand towards IT-only.
Domain/trademark risk: **High.** **"Start IT" is ITIDA's flagship national startup incubation programme in Egypt** (run by TIEC since 2013, 47+ cohorts). ITIDA is the government agency for your exact sector. Expect confusion, weak registrability, and possible objection. Also used by Start it @KBC (Belgian accelerator) and StartIT (Kyiv IT training centre). "Start" + "IT" is descriptive, so distinctiveness is low.
Fits the audience: Partially. Café and shop owners would read it as an IT firm, not the partner for their menus and ads.

**Score: 4/10.** The insight is right; the name isn't usable.
