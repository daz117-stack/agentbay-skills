# Firststone: Messaging Framework

Built with the `brand-messaging` skill from `brand-context.md` and `positioning.md`. Arabic lines are written for Egyptian audiences: polite colloquial, not formal MSA.

## 01 — Core message

**Firststone helps small cafés, restaurants and shops start right and run smoothly, by setting up how they look and how they work, and staying to keep it that way.**

## 02 — Value proposition

Firststone is one small team that sets up a business's menus, ads and screens *and* the simple systems behind them: orders between counter and kitchen, stock on the shelf. Everything is designed to look professional, built to run every day, and priced in clear packages a small business can afford. When it's live, we don't disappear. We support it, update it and keep it working.

## 03 — Tagline options

| Tagline | Style | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Start here. Start right.** · «ابدأ من هنا… ابدأ صح» | Functional + confident | The founders' own insight ("start here") plus the promise ("start right"). It works as a sign-off, a CTA and a film end line. |
| Start right. Run steady. | Functional | Covers both halves (setup + reliability) and maps to Stable. Less ownable. |
| Built right, from the first stone. · «مبني صح، من أول حجر» | Aspirational | Explains the name. Better as a story line than a tagline. |
| Every business starts somewhere. Start solid. | Emotional | Warm and inclusive, but long. |
| Looks right. Works right. | Witty / functional | Neatly says media + systems. Slightly generic. |

**Recommended: "Start here. Start right." / «ابدأ من هنا… ابدأ صح»**
It keeps the founders' "start here" idea, adds the benefit customers actually buy (getting it *right*), and works natively in Egyptian Arabic, where «ابدأ صح» is something people genuinely say. It also pairs with the name: *Firststone. Start here. Start right.*

## 04 — Messaging hierarchy

**Level 1, Brand headline**
> **Start here. Start right.**

**Level 2, Supporting statement**
> Firststone sets up how your business looks and how it runs (menus, ads, orders and stock) and then stays to keep it working. One team, clear prices, no surprises.

**Level 3, Key messages**

1. **One team for your look and your systems.** *(Well-designed)*
   Your menu, your ads and the screens and software they run on are made by the same people, so they always match and work together. No chasing three different suppliers.
   *Proof:* The same team designs the menu and builds the system that displays it.

2. **Built to run every single day.** *(Stable)*
   We test before go-live, train your staff and plan for the bad day: rush hour, a dropped connection. Simple tools that keep working are worth more than clever tools that don't.
   *Proof:* A written go-live checklist and a defined fallback for each system *(to be set up)*.

3. **Clear prices. Clear status. No surprises.** *(Trusted)*
   You know what you're paying before we start, what's included, and where your job stands. Your designs and data are yours.
   *Proof:* Published packages and written scope *(to be set up)*.

4. **Sized for small businesses.** *(Affordable)*
   Packages built for one shop, not a chain: start with what you need and add the rest when you're ready.
   *Proof:* Entry package price *(to be defined)*.

**Level 4, Proof-point bank** *(collect and verify; no item may be used publicly until true)*
- Entry setup package from **[price] EGP**, monthly plan from **[price] EGP**
- Support response: **[same business day]** on WhatsApp
- Go-live checklist: tested, documented, staff trained, fallback defined
- **[N]** Cairo businesses set up *(after launch, with permission)*
- One founder's professional background in IT service and change management *(check employer policy before public use)*
- Client data and design files handed over on request, no lock-in

## 05 — Audience-specific messaging

**Café / restaurant owners (primary)**
- Care most about: rush-hour chaos, lost orders, menus with wrong prices, looking as good as the big places.
- Lead with: *"Your menu, your orders, your look: set up right, in one go."*
- Proofs that land hardest: a working demo on a real tablet; a before/after menu; the support promise.
- Avoid: tech jargon (API, cloud, integration), "digital transformation".

**Supermarket / retail owners**
- Care most about: stock running out, wrong shelf prices, theft and mistakes, slow cashiers.
- Lead with: *"Know what's on your shelves, and show it off properly."*
- Proofs: a stock screen showing what to reorder; price tags and screens that update together.
- Avoid: overselling ERP; promising accounting or tax features you don't support.

**Partners: hardware sellers, POS resellers, suppliers (secondary)**
- Care about: referrals, not being competed against.
- Lead with: *"We set up the look and the simple tools around your products."*
- Avoid: positioning against their products.

**Gulf small chains (later, phase 2)**
- Lead with reliability and consistency across branches. Use more formal Arabic (Gulf-neutral MSA-light).

## 07 — Things not to say

- **No unprovable claims:** "the best in Egypt", "guaranteed sales growth", "100% uptime", fake client counts or results.
- **No category clichés:** "digital transformation", "cutting-edge", "solutions provider", "one-stop shop", "AI-powered" (unless literally true and relevant).
- **No competitor confusion:** don't call it a "POS" or "ERP" in headlines. That invites a feature comparison with Foodics or BIM POS you won't win. Say "order system", "stock screen".
- **Nothing that contradicts the position:** no "cheapest prices", no countdown urgency, no "enterprise-grade" language.
- **Not "Start IT":** keep IT out of the tagline. It narrows the brand and echoes ITIDA's programme.
