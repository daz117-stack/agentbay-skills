# Firststone: Voice Guide

Built with the `brand-voice` skill from `brand-context.md`, `positioning.md` and `messaging.md`. Firststone writes in **three registers**: Egyptian colloquial Arabic (social, WhatsApp, sales), light MSA (formal documents, Gulf), and English (website, decks, interface terms). The voice is the same in all three.

## 01 — Voice overview

Firststone talks like the dependable technician every shop owner wishes they had, the one who also happens to have taste. It is calm when the customer is stressed, plain when others use jargon, and specific when others make big claims. It respects that the owner runs a real business with real pressure, so it gets to the point, explains what will happen, and never talks down. Warmth comes from being helpful and present, not from exclamation marks.

**Voice essence:** *Firststone sounds like a calm, capable neighbour who sets things up properly: clear, warm, and exact.*

## 02 — Tone dimensions

**Formality**: Casual ←●——→ Formal
Casual side, never sloppy. Talk like a respected professional across the counter.
> "We'll set it up on Tuesday and train your staff before opening."

**Energy**: Calm ●———→ Energetic
Calm, always. Confidence comes from certainty, not volume.
> "It's done. Here's what changed."

**Humour**: Serious ——●——→ Witty
Light and situational, never at the customer's expense. No humour in support or billing.
> "Rush hour is chaos enough. Your order screen shouldn't add to it."

**Expertise**: Accessible ●———→ Authoritative
Expert underneath, accessible on the surface. Explain outcomes, not technology.
> "When the kitchen marks an order ready, the counter sees it instantly."

**Warmth**: Distant ——●——→ Intimate
Warm and personal ("we", "you", names), but not over-familiar.
> «أي حاجة تقف، كلّمنا على واتساب ونرد عليك في نفس اليوم.» ("If anything stops working, message us on WhatsApp and we'll reply the same day.")

## 03 — Voice qualities

**Plain**
We use the customer's words, not ours. If a shop owner wouldn't say it, we don't write it.
- **Do:** "Your menu prices update on every screen at once."
- **Don't:** "Real-time multi-endpoint content synchronisation."

**Calm**
We lower the temperature. Problems are stated, owned and solved, without drama or panic.
- **Do:** "The kitchen screen lost connection at 1:40. Orders were saved and showed up when it reconnected. We've added a backup connection."
- **Don't:** "CRITICAL OUTAGE!!! We're so sorry!!!"

**Exact**
Specific beats impressive. Times, prices, steps, names.
- **Do:** "Setup takes 3 days. Day 1: menu photos. Day 2: install. Day 3: staff training."
- **Don't:** "Fast, seamless onboarding."

**Warm**
We're on the owner's side and it shows: we notice their business and remember details.
- **Do:** "Your weekend breakfast is selling. Want it on the main screen until 12?"
- **Don't:** "Dear valued customer, please be informed…"

**Honest**
We say what something can't do, and say "no" when it's not needed.
- **Do:** "You don't need a stock system yet. A shared sheet will do for one shelf."
- **Don't:** "Our powerful platform handles everything."

**Practical**
Every message points to the next useful action.
- **Do:** "Send us your current menu photo and we'll show you a draft by Thursday."
- **Don't:** "Contact us to learn more about our offerings."

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Words we use:** set up · start right · run · work · simple · clear · steady · on time · ready · your place · your staff · your menu · the counter / the kitchen · tested · handed over · we'll stay with you · fixed price · «ابدأ صح» · «مظبوط» · «واضح» · «جاهز» · «نجهّز» · «مكانك» · «شغلك»

**Words we avoid:** solutions · leverage · synergy · cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · revolutionary · game-changing · world-class · seamless · robust · disrupt · digital transformation · AI-powered (unless literal) · one-stop shop · "valued customer" · over-formal MSA in casual channels («نحيطكم علماً», «يسعدنا إبلاغكم»)

**Brand-specific language:**
- Customers are **clients** in documents and **you / your place («مكانك»)** in conversation. Never "users" in marketing.
- What we sell: **setup** (the first job) and **plans** (monthly support/content). Not "projects" or "tickets" in client-facing copy.
- Products by what they do: "order screen", "stock screen", "digital menu". Not "POS", "ERP" or "CMS" in headlines.
- Name usage: **Firststone**, one word, capital F. Never "FirstStone", "First Stone" or "FS". Arabic: per the identity decision (see `identity.md`).

## 05 — Writing style rules

- **Sentence length:** short. Average 8–14 words. One idea per sentence. Mix in one longer sentence when explaining a process.
- **Punctuation:** full stops are the brand. Exclamation marks are banned in product, support and billing, and allowed at most once in a social post. No ALL CAPS sentences (the mono eyebrow style in design is the exception). Em dashes are sparing; prefer a full stop.
- **Capitalisation:** sentence case everywhere, including headlines and buttons. Product names are lower-case descriptors ("order screen").
- **Numbers:** always numerals for prices, times, quantities, days ("3 days", "85 EGP", "12:40"). Arabic channels use Western numerals for prices and times (more legible on screens). Check per client.
- **Contractions:** yes in English ("we'll", "it's"). They keep the warmth.
- **Voice:** active. "We'll install it", not "it will be installed". Passive only when blame would be unhelpful ("the connection dropped").
- **Humour:** light and situational in social only. Never in support, outages or invoices.
- **Emoji:** at most one per WhatsApp/social message, functional (👋 ✅ 📍). None in documents or interfaces.
- **Arabic register:** Egyptian colloquial («هنجهّز», «كلّمنا», «جاهز») for social, WhatsApp and sales. Light MSA for contracts, invoices and Gulf audiences. Never mix registers within one sentence. English tech terms only when the owner already uses them ("WiFi", "QR").

## 06 — Channel adaptations

| Channel | Tone adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website homepage | Confident, spare | "Start here. Start right. We set up how your place looks and runs, then stay to keep it working." |
| Social media | Warmer, local, visual-first | «المنيو بتاعك يستاهل يبان أحسن. ابعتلنا صورته ونوريك الفرق.» ("Your menu deserves to look better. Send us a photo and we'll show you the difference.") |
| Email / proposals | Precise, structured | "Scope: digital menu (2 screens), order screen (counter + kitchen). Price: [X] EGP. Live by: 14 Nov." |
| Customer support (WhatsApp) | Calm, fast, human | «وصلتنا رسالتك. هنبص عليها دلوقتي ونرد عليك قبل ٣.» ("Got your message. We're looking at it now and will reply before 3.") |
| Interface / error copy | Shortest, reassuring, actionable | "No connection. Orders are saved and will send automatically." |

## 07 — Before and after

**Before:** "We are a leading provider of innovative digital solutions empowering businesses through cutting-edge technology."
**After:** "We set up your menu, your ads and your order screens, and keep them working."
**Why it works:** It says what we actually do, in the customer's words.

**Before:** "Unfortunately, due to unforeseen technical difficulties, the service was temporarily unavailable."
**After:** "Your kitchen screen was offline from 1:40 to 1:55. No orders were lost. We've added a backup connection."
**Why it works:** Specific, calm, owns the fix.

**Before:** «يسعدنا إبلاغ سيادتكم بأنه قد تم الانتهاء من تنفيذ المشروع.» ("We are pleased to inform you that execution of the project has been completed.")
**After:** «كله جاهز. نعدّي عليك بكرة ١١ الصبح ندرّب الفريق.» ("All ready. We'll come by tomorrow at 11 to train the team.")
**Why it works:** Colloquial warmth, concrete next step, no bureaucracy.

**Before:** "Our robust, scalable platform supports unlimited SKUs!!"
**After:** "Add as many products as you stock. The screen tells you what to reorder."
**Why it works:** The outcome replaces the spec; the hype goes.

**Before:** "Contact us today for an amazing offer!!!"
**After:** "Send us a photo of your menu. We'll send a free draft by Thursday."
**Why it works:** A concrete, low-risk next step instead of pressure.

## 08 — Things we never say

- Hollow hype: "world-class", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "best in Egypt", "#1".
- Promises we can't keep: "100% uptime", "guaranteed sales", "never breaks".
- Over-apology or panic in incidents. We state, own and fix.
- Corporate MSA boilerplate in casual channels («نحيط سيادتكم علماً»).
- Pressure tactics: countdowns, "last chance", fake scarcity.
- Talking down: "it's easy, anyone can do it", or explaining the owner's own business to them.
- Competitor-bashing by name.
