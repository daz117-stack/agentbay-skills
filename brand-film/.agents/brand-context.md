## Audience
- **Primary Audience**: Owners and managers of independent and small-chain restaurants, cafés, bakeries, supermarkets/mini-markets and retail shops, first in Egypt (Greater Cairo first **[confirm]**), then the Gulf (KSA/UAE). Usually the owner decides, often hands-on in the shop. Price-sensitive, time-poor, burned by unreliable freelancers or locked into one-size platforms.
- **Secondary**: Small multi-branch operators who outgrow paper, WhatsApp and spreadsheets but can't justify enterprise systems.
- **Key Problem**: The business runs on memory, paper slips, WhatsApp messages and outdated printed prices. Its visuals look improvised next to bigger competitors. Existing options are either cheap but unreliable (freelancers, one-off templates) or capable but rigid and costly (regional POS/menu platforms on subscription).
- **Their Language** **[confirm through 5–10 owner interviews; these are hypotheses, not quotes]**: "I need something simple my staff can use", "the last guy disappeared after delivery", "orders get lost between the counter and the kitchen", "I change prices and the menu is wrong", "how much per month?", "does it work if the internet drops?", "I want it to look like the big places". Expect most conversations in Egyptian Arabic, with English software terms mixed in.

## Positioning
- **Differentiation**:
  1. **One partner for media + systems.** The menu design, the ad, and the screen and software that show it come from the same team, so they stay consistent and connected.
  2. **Built to be dependable.** Bank-grade process discipline (change control, testing, support) applied to small-business tools. Stable is a promise, not decoration.
  3. **Simple on purpose.** Tools sized to the actual operational need, not feature-heavy platforms.
  4. **Affordable without looking cheap.** Design quality of an agency at small-business prices.
- **Competitors** (desk research, Oct 2026; verify before external use):
  - *Regional platforms*: Foodics (restaurant POS + payments, in Egypt since 2020, acquired POSRocket), BIM POS / Compuware (restaurant and retail POS, QR menus), elmenus (food discovery, digitised menus).
  - *Point solutions*: QR/digital-menu vendors such as eMenu, QRMenuPro, Billqode.
  - *Low-cost alternatives*: freelancers on Khamsat / Mostaql (QR menu setups from ~$20), local print shops and small social-media agencies.
- **Market Position**: Value / volume, meaning affordable and packaged, with premium-feeling execution. Not a luxury agency, not a bargain freelancer.
- **Strategic tension to manage**: The volume pricing has to be backed by productised, repeatable packages (templates, standard setups, monthly plans). Otherwise "affordable + well-designed + custom" becomes unprofitable.

## Values & Mission

- **Mission** (working draft **[confirm]**): Give everyday businesses in Egypt and the Gulf the look and the systems of a well-run company: simple, dependable, and within reach.

## Goals
- **Primary Goal** (12 months, **[confirm]**): Win the first paying clients in Greater Cairo and turn at least 3 into documented, permission-granted case studies. Establish recurring revenue (monthly software/support plans) alongside one-off media work.
- **Key Metrics** **[confirm]**: Active paying clients · monthly recurring revenue · client retention / churn · referral share of new clients · support response and uptime of delivered systems.
