## Version 1: The long story (About page)

It's 1 p.m. on a Friday at **[a café in Heliopolis / a relative's shop / a place you know]**. There's a queue at the counter. The kitchen is working from paper slips, and one of them is on the floor. The menu on the wall still says the latte costs 70; it's been 85 for a month, so every second customer argues about it. The owner is answering WhatsApp messages from people asking whether their order is ready, and she can't tell which order they mean.

None of this is unusual. That's the problem.

Most small businesses in Egypt run on memory, paper and WhatsApp. Not because owners don't care, but because the options available to them don't fit. A freelancer can make a nice menu for a few hundred pounds, but disappears when it needs updating. A big platform can run a restaurant chain, but it's built for chains: priced for them, and focused on one job. Nobody sets up the whole thing (how the place looks *and* how it runs) and then stays.

We noticed this from two directions. **[Fady]** spends his working life making sure complex systems change safely and keep running: change control, testing, support. That's the unglamorous discipline that keeps services up when it matters. **[Hamdy: background, e.g. design / media / development / retail operations, and what he saw from his side.]** **[The moment you decided: e.g. "When we set up a simple order screen for X and saw the difference on the first busy day…"]**

So we started Firststone. The name is the idea: the first stone is the one everything else rests on. Get it right and the rest stands up. In Arabic we say «وضع حجر الأساس», laying the foundation stone, when something begins properly. That's what we do for small businesses.

Firststone sets up how your place looks (menus, screens, ads, the visuals your customers see) and how it runs: orders that move from counter to kitchen without getting lost, stock you can see before it runs out, prices that are right on every screen at once. One team does all of it, so it all fits together.

We keep it simple on purpose. A café doesn't need enterprise software; it needs an order screen that works at rush hour. A mini-market doesn't need a dashboard of forty charts; it needs to know what to reorder. We build what the business needs, explain it in plain words, and train the people who'll use it.

And we stay. Every setup is tested before it goes live, handed over with training, and supported afterwards. Prices are clear before we start. Your designs and your data are yours.

This is for the owner who runs the place, who is on the floor at rush hour and knows every regular by name, and who is ready to stop improvising without paying for something built for someone else.

## Version 2: The short story (homepage / pitch)

Most small businesses run on paper slips, WhatsApp and a menu with last month's prices. The options don't fit: freelancers make it look nice and then disappear, and big platforms are built for chains.

Firststone exists to set small businesses up properly, the first stone everything else rests on. We set up how your place looks (menus, screens, ads) and how it runs (orders from counter to kitchen, stock you can see, prices right everywhere). One team does it all, so it fits together.

We keep it simple on purpose, explain it in plain words, and train your staff. Every setup is tested before go-live, priced clearly before we start, and supported afterwards. Your designs and data stay yours.

We're **[Fady and Hamdy]**: one of us has spent years keeping critical systems running, the other **[Hamdy's strength]**. Together we build the setup we wish every small business had.

- **Customer as hero:** the owner on the floor at rush hour. Firststone is the guide.
