## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Firststone sets up how a small café, restaurant or shop looks and how it runs (menus, ads, order and stock tools), then stays to keep it working.
**Launch product (built for 3amo Fouad; printed menu + QR live, ordering site live but not yet reachable from the QR):** **QR menu + WhatsApp ordering.** A print-ready menu (PDF) carrying a QR code, and a mobile menu page in Arabic with category tabs, search, item sizes, a cart that survives a page reload, pickup or delivery, and a **Send on WhatsApp** button that opens the restaurant's own WhatsApp with an itemized order, quantities and total already written (*simple version*). 3amo Fouad's live site runs a *server version*: the order and its confirmed total are saved on the server, WhatsApp carries only a reference, and staff look the order up behind a sign-in. **Which version is the default offer: [NEED, founders].** No app for the diner to install, no hardware for the shop, no per-order fee from Firststone.
**What it does (full promise):** One small team designs the visuals a customer sees (digital and printed menus, product ads, social content) and sets up the simple systems behind them (orders moving from counter to kitchen, stock on the shelf, prices that match on every screen). After go-live it supports, updates and keeps the setup running on a monthly plan.
**Product category:** "The setup partner" for small businesses. Owners will not search for that phrase. They search for, or ask a friend about, *digital menu*, *QR menu*, *منيو ديجيتال*, *a designer for my menu*, *a system for orders*, *a program for the shop*. Copy names the category; SEO and ads target the search terms.
**Product type:** Productized service with recurring plans (setup package + monthly plan). Some delivered tools are software, but the buyer is buying a done-for-you service.
**Business model:** One-off **setup packages** (fixed scope, fixed price) plus **monthly plans** (support, content updates, menu and price changes). Recurring plans are the business; setup is the entry point. Prices **[NEED]**, see `../marketing/02-offer-and-pricing.md`.

## Target Audience
**First-fit segment:** takeaway- and delivery-heavy food places (foul, ta'meya, grills, sandwiches, bakeries) that already take orders by phone or WhatsApp. 3amo Fouad (New Cairo + North Coast branches) is this profile.
**Target companies (full range):** Independent cafés, restaurants, bakeries, mini-markets and retail shops with 1–5 branches in Greater Cairo **[confirm city focus]**. Gulf (KSA/UAE) is phase 2 and is kept out of launch messaging.
**Decision-makers:** The owner, usually hands-on in the shop. In larger units, an owner-manager pair where the manager feels the daily pain and the owner signs off on spend.
**Primary use case:** Getting the business out of improvisation: wrong menu prices, lost paper order slips, orders arriving over WhatsApp, visuals that look home-made next to the chains.
**Jobs to be done:**
- "Make my place look as professional as the big places, without paying what they pay."
- "Stop orders and prices going wrong at rush hour."
- "Give me one person to call when something needs changing or stops working."
**Use cases:**
- A café opening or rebranding needs a menu (screen + print), social visuals and a price list that stays correct.
- A restaurant moving kitchen tickets off paper onto a simple order screen.
- A mini-market that wants a stock screen showing what to reorder, plus shelf-price and promo visuals.
- Seasonal moments: Ramadan iftar/suhoor menus, Eid offers, summer menus.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** The business runs on memory, paper slips, WhatsApp and printed prices that went out of date weeks ago. Its visuals look improvised.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Freelancers (Khamsat, Mostaql, Instagram designers): cheap and fast, then unreachable when the menu needs changing.
- Small agencies and print shops: visuals only; nobody touches how the place runs.
- QR-menu vendors: one product, sold and left.
- Foodics, BIM POS: capable systems, priced and built for growth-stage chains; no design or marketing.
- Doing nothing: free until the next lost order or price argument.
**What it costs them:** Lost and wrong orders at rush hour, arguments over prices, staff time spent re-writing menus, and customers who judge the place on how it looks online. **[NEED: real cost examples from interviews]**
**Emotional tension:** Embarrassment ("it looks home-made"), fatigue at chasing suppliers, fear of paying monthly for something that breaks or that staff won't use.

## Competitive Landscape
*(Desk research, Oct 2026. Re-verify before any public comparison. Never name competitors in ads or posts.)*
**Direct:** QR/digital-menu vendors (eMenu, QRMenuPro, Billqode). They fall short because they sell one product and stop there.
**Secondary:** Foodics, BIM POS / Compuware. They fall short for a 1–5-branch shop because the price and feature set target chains, and they don't handle the shop's visuals.
**Ordering alternatives:** delivery apps (e.g., Talabat, elmenus ordering) bring new diners but take a commission on each order **[verify current Egyptian commission levels before any comparison]**; a QR ordering page brings repeat and walk-in customers' orders straight to the shop's WhatsApp for a flat fee. Position it alongside the apps, never against them.
**Indirect:** Freelancers and print shops (one-off, not there next month); a nephew who "knows computers"; paper and WhatsApp.

- Looks + runs + stays: the same team designs the menu, sets up the screen or order tool it runs on, and supports it.
- Simple on purpose: tools sized to the shop, explained in plain words, with staff trained at handover.
- Published package prices and written scope **[NEED: packages and prices]**.
- Built to run every day: tested before go-live, with a defined fallback if the internet drops **[NEED: go-live checklist and fallback per product]**.
**How we do it differently:** Productized setup packages rather than custom projects, then a monthly plan that keeps everything current.
**Why that's better:** The owner deals with one team, pays a known price, and the menu, ads and screens always match.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "How much per month?" | Answer with the number, in the first message. Packages are fixed and written. **[NEED: prices]** |
| "A freelancer will do the menu for a few hundred pounds." | True for a one-off design. Our price includes setting it up on your screens, changing prices when you need, and someone who answers next month. |
| "Does it work if the internet drops?" | The menu page opens on the customer's own mobile data, so the shop's WiFi doesn't matter, and the printed menu carries the same prices. If WhatsApp is down, the phone number is on the menu. **[verify: page hosting uptime and load time on a weak connection]** |
| "My staff won't use it." | We train your team on handover day and keep the tool to what they need. |
| "What if you disappear too?" | Written support response time, and your designs and data are yours to take. **[NEED: SLA a two-person team can keep]** |
| "I already have Foodics / a POS." | Keep it. We set up the look around it (menus, screens, ads). |
| "I'm already on Talabat." | Keep it for new diners. The QR menu is for your regulars and walk-ins to order straight to your WhatsApp, with no commission per order. |
| "Customers won't scan a QR." | They don't have to download anything; the page opens in the phone's browser. The printed menu with the same prices stays on the table. |

**Anti-persona:** Chains of 10+ branches that need enterprise POS, integrations and accounting; owners shopping for the cheapest one-off design; anyone wanting guaranteed sales numbers or viral growth.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** A busy-day failure (lost orders, a price row), a price change that makes the printed menu wrong, a competitor that opened looking polished, the last freelancer going quiet.
**Pull:** Looking like the big places, one number to call, a fixed price, a working demo on a real tablet.
**Habit:** "Paper works well enough", WhatsApp orders are familiar, the owner's nephew already does the Instagram.
**Anxiety:** Monthly cost creeping up, staff not adopting it, systems failing at rush hour, getting locked in.

## Brand Voice
*(Full guide: `voice.md`.)*
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Plain | The owner's words, outcomes over technology | Dumbed down | "Your menu prices update on every screen at once." |
| Calm | State, own and fix problems | Cold or robotic | "The kitchen screen lost connection at 1:40. Orders were saved." |
| Exact | Numbers, days, steps, names | Dense spec sheets | "Setup takes 3 days. Day 1: menu photos. Day 2: install. Day 3: training." |
| Warm | On the owner's side, notices their business | Over-familiar, emoji-heavy | "Your weekend breakfast is selling. Want it on the main screen until 12?" |
| Honest | Says what something can't do | Self-deprecating | "You don't need a stock system yet. A shared sheet will do." |

**Tone by context:**
- Support: calm, fast, a time commitment in every reply. No humour, no exclamation marks.
- Launch: confident and spare. Show the work (before/after), don't announce greatness.
- Errors: shortest possible, reassuring, actionable ("No connection. Orders are saved and will send automatically.").
- Sales: practical; price, scope, timeline, what happens after go-live.
- Social: warmer, Egyptian colloquial, visual-first, at most one emoji.
**Registers:** Egyptian colloquial Arabic for social, WhatsApp and sales; light MSA for contracts and Gulf; English for website and decks. Never mix registers in one sentence.
**Terms we own:** "Start here. Start right." · «ابدأ من هنا… ابدأ صح» · "the setup partner" · «متجهّز مع Firststone»
**Never use (and why):** "best in Egypt", "#1" (unprovable); "guaranteed sales", "100% uptime" (can't keep it); "cheapest" (contradicts the position); "Start IT" (ITIDA's programme); "FirstStone", "First Stone", "FS" (the name is **Firststone**, one word, capital F).
